Automating short-form video work

Reliable AI Media
Automation Pipeline

This tool pulls topics from RSS feeds, prepares content, generates videos and passes them to publishing integrations. I also worked on deduplication, retries and notifications, because a scheduled tool isn't much use if a failure goes unnoticed.

01 / What it currently does

These are the content categories, publishing integrations and execution options currently in the code.

4content profiles: tech, consumer money, cybersecurity and science future
3publishing interfaces: YouTube, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels
2notification channels: email and Telegram
3safe execution controls: dry-run, no-upload and privacy override

02 / From topic to video

It finds a topic, prepares the content, generates a video and records the publishing result.

03 / What happens when something fails

Don't make the same video twiceseen.json tracks processed topics so the same story isn't picked again from a different feed.
Keep videos that failed to uploadpending_upload.json remembers generated files that didn't upload. The next run retries them first instead of generating them again.
Find where it brokeEach run records the topic, source URL, result and the step where any error happened.
Logs and messagesRuns, platform upload results and daily reports are logged separately, with email or Telegram notifications available.

05 / What I didn't build from scratch

The core video-generation code comes from an existing open-source project.

I used the portable MoneyPrinterTurbo project as the video-generation base. My work is around it: RSS feeds, topic selection rules, deduplication, upload recovery, scheduling, logs and notifications. I didn't write the underlying video engine from scratch.

06 / What isn't finished yet

Automatic quality checks aren't connected to the publishing step yet.

config.example.toml has quality-threshold settings, but the main workflow doesn't enforce them before publishing. For now I can use --dry-run or --no-upload to review a topic or video before deciding to publish.

07 / What matters most to me here

I want to know what happened when an automated run fails.

That's why I kept records of where a story came from, what was generated, whether the upload worked and what still needs attention. It makes the next debugging session much easier.
