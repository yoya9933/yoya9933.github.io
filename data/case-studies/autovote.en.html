AutoVoteShareholder Voting Record Assistant

Some tasks aren't difficult, but repeating them takes time. Looking up shareholder voting records is one of them: sign in, find the right meeting, save a receipt, then organize everything for review or printing. I built AutoVote to cut down on those repetitive steps so people can focus on the parts that actually need their attention.

This is an ongoing project focused on retrieving and organizing existing voting receipts. The feature for submitting new votes is not enabled on the public portal.

Why I built it

I wanted to make a small but repetitive part of the work easier.

When handling shareholder-gift paperwork, there are situations where electronic voting records need to be checked or prepared for printing. Doing this manually means signing into the official site, finding each company and meeting, saving the receipt, and arranging the files. With multiple records, even these simple steps add up, and it's easy to miss entries on another page.

That's why I started building AutoVote. The goal isn't to decide how anyone should vote. It's to handle the repetitive looking-up, organizing and exporting that software can do more consistently.

How it helps

Turn scattered steps into a more manageable workflow.

Less manual searching

With the account holder's authorization, the tool looks up meetings that already have voting records instead of requiring repeated manual navigation.

Receipts in one place

It organizes existing receipts by company and meeting and checks record counts across pages to avoid missing or duplicated entries.

Files ready to use

Receipts can be saved individually, bundled in a ZIP or prepared as an A4 PDF for batch printing.

Personal records stay protected

Users sign in and give permission first. Certificates and stored receipts are encrypted, and access is restricted to the relevant account.

Where it stands today

22 existing receipts retrieved locally. Unverified features are still labeled as such.

On 2026-11-22, I used an authorized account locally to retrieve 22 existing official voting receipts from two pages, checked the record counts and prepared images and print files. Real Google sign-in has also been tested on the public portal. The full public upload, lookup and download flow still needs live validation.

New-ballot submission remains disabled. Automated abstention voting has been implemented and tested with simulated scenarios, but it has not been validated with an eligible live ballot. I can't claim the system has successfully submitted a new real-world vote.

What mattered to me while building it

Permission comes first

Voting records and digital certificates aren't things to handle casually. Users must authenticate and authorize access. Google login and Windows DPAPI encryption protect account and locally stored sensitive data.

Stop when something is unclear

If an official page changes, records don't match, or a voting result is uncertain, the program should stop rather than guess or resubmit.

The public portal connects to a local computer and is unavailable when that machine is off or sleeping. This case study contains no customer certificates, personal records, real receipts or private source code. Voting and shareholder-gift collection remain subject to applicable rules.
